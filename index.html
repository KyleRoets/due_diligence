    What is ReachLMS, and how does it relate to Canvas?

    ReachLMS is the open-source Canvas LMS hosted, extended and supported by YouMultiply. Faculty and students get the same Canvas interface they already know. On top of the open-source core, YouMultiply adds:

    • In-line annotation of submissions
    • A white-labeled mobile app with push notifications and offline study
    • An e-commerce add-on with payments and certificates
    • An interactive HTML5 content hub and video tools
    • Reach AI, for querying student and course data in plain English

    Around the platform come hosting in your chosen region, migration, training and support. ReachLMS tracks upstream Canvas rather than forking it, so every Canvas improvement reaches you.

    Why does the open-source license matter?

    Canvas is released under the AGPLv3 open-source license, which is what makes ReachLMS possible.

    Because ReachLMS builds on Canvas, you keep the freedom to run the core Canvas software independently should you ever need to. That gives you a practical fallback and reduces your dependence on any single provider.

    Can instructors annotate directly on student submissions?

    Yes. The SpeedGrader annotation tool lets instructors highlight, comment and draw directly on PDF submissions. Every annotation is linked to a comment in the assignment sidebar, so the student is notified; in the hosted DocViewer, annotations were harder for students to find. The annotated document is saved automatically for the student to view and download.

    For in-line annotation, set the assignment to accept PDF. Every word processor exports to PDF in one step.

    What happens to quizzes? Do we keep New Quizzes?

    You keep your quizzes. Both New and Classic Quizzes content is migrated. ReachLMS uses Classic Quizzes, which keeps a survey tool and the per-attempt quiz log that academic-integrity processes may rely on. Interactive HTML5 content and LTI assessment tools add more question types.

    New Quizzes has three types Classic lacks: categorization, ordering and hot spot. These are rebuilt as interactive HTML5 items during migration; everything else converts automatically via QTI. Deeper automatic grade pass-back is on the roadmap.

    What replaces Commons for sharing content?

    Is there a mobile app? What changes for students?

    Yes. Use the standard ReachLMS app on the Apple and Google stores, or a white-labeled app with your institution’s name, icon and branding. Both support core Canvas functionality, push notifications and an offline mode that downloads course content for study without a connection.

    Instructure’s Canvas Student and Teacher apps do not work with ReachLMS. Students use the ReachLMS app, the white-labeled app or the responsive mobile web. Templates are supplied at cutover, and switching takes a student under a minute.

    What exactly is the white-labeled app?

    A newly designed app built from the ground up, not a re-skin. Students see courses, modules, assignments and due dates, submit work, view grades and feedback, join discussions and use the inbox and calendar. Instructors can grade, post announcements and join discussions. Observer and parent access follows web permissions.

    It is published under YouMultiply’s developer accounts, so you need no Apple or Google enrollment; your own account is available for a fee. Teach Every Nation’s app, TEN Hub, is an example.

    How is the app priced, and how long does it take?

    $3,500 setup and $2,400 per year, with no per-user or per-download charge. The annual fee covers store listings, operating-system updates and feature updates.

    Order to store availability is typically four to six weeks, mostly Apple’s and Google’s review time. You supply your logo, brand colors, a short store description and a privacy policy link.

    How far can we brand the web platform?

    Branding is done for you during implementation: logo, colors, login page and your own domain (for example learn.yourinstitution.edu), with SSL provisioned and renewed for you. Administrators get a Theme Editor walkthrough, and custom CSS and JavaScript are supported. Notification emails come from your own domain once DNS records are added.

    Faculties, campuses or partner schools can each have their own theme at sub-account level. Sharing an instance costs nothing extra.

    How does the architecture reduce the risk of a large breach?

    Every institution runs on its own dedicated, isolated instance with its own database and application servers. A compromise of one instance cannot expose another institution’s data. On a shared multi-tenant platform, one breach can affect every customer at once, as the sector saw in 2026.

    Patching is automated across instances, and critical upstream patches reach all of them within 72 hours. Only the shared tier for very small institutions shares an instance; anyone with more than a few hundred users gets a dedicated one.

    Where and how is our data hosted?

    On DigitalOcean, whose datacenters hold SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certifications. You choose from 13 regions across North America, Europe, Asia and Australia. There is no region in Africa or the Middle East; Frankfurt or London is recommended, with a global CDN keeping things fast.

    Traffic is encrypted with TLS 1.2 or later and data is encrypted at rest. The CDN layer adds DDoS protection, firewall rules and rate limiting, and server access is limited to named engineers.

    How do you find and fix vulnerabilities?

    Critical upstream security patches are deployed to all instances within 72 hours of release; routine updates follow the normal release cycle. All staff access to infrastructure requires MFA.

    Regular vulnerability scanning and hardening are carried out, and independent penetration testing is planned for 2027. Researchers can report issues to security@youmultiply.com, with acknowledgement within two business days.

    How is access to the platform and our data controlled?

    Single sign-on through SAML 2.0, OpenID Connect and OAuth, CAS and LDAP covers Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Okta and campus providers. MFA can be required for administrators or all users, and your administrators control granular roles.

    YouMultiply staff use least-privilege access with MFA and are vetted before production access. Any “act as user” session is recorded in the audit log, and authentication, grade-change and course logs are kept for twelve months.

    What happens if there is a security incident?

    You are notified within 24 hours of an incident being confirmed (a contractual commitment), with a named contact and a written post-incident report. The response plan covers monitoring, containment, communication, recovery from separately held backups, and review. No ransoms are paid.

    There is no cyber liability insurance currently. Protection comes from instance isolation, backup design, 24-hour notification and a data-protection indemnity. There have been no data breaches to date.

    What certifications and assessments do you hold?

    The hosting provider, DigitalOcean, holds SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001.

    A HECVAT is on the priority list and will be fast-tracked if it blocks your procurement. An independent penetration test is scheduled for 2027 with shareable results, and ISO 27001 certification is targeted for the end of 2027.

    What are our responsibilities as the client?

    Security is shared. YouMultiply covers the platform, infrastructure, patching, backups, monitoring and its own staff’s access.

    Your institution covers the identity provider and its password and MFA policies, administrator accounts and prompt removal of access, vetting LTI tools, how users share data in courses, and user awareness. Every implementation includes an administrator security checklist, and access-review reports are available on request.

    Why is moving from Canvas lower risk than a typical migration?

    ReachLMS is Canvas. The data model, export format, API, SIS import format and interface are identical, so content moves without being rebuilt and faculty do not retrain.

    A move from hosted Canvas typically takes two to eight weeks from signed order to go-live, against six to twelve months for a conventional LMS migration.

    Exactly what is migrated, and what is not?

    • Automatic: courses and content, files, quizzes (Classic and New), rubrics, outcomes, calendar events, users and enrollments, terms, sub-accounts, roles and your theme.
    • Full implementation: your database is transmuted where available, bringing gradebook history, submissions, inbox messages and discussion posts. Otherwise an API export brings grades and submissions but not inbox messages.
    • Re-created: LTI registrations, developer keys, SSO and notification preferences.
    • Special handling: Commons exported, Studio media re-hosted, Catalog rebuilt.
    • Not migrated: historical analytics and student ePortfolios.

    Can you migrate our entire database, or is it course by course?

    Both. A database export from your provider is imported directly and preserves the most history. Otherwise an automated API export of courses, users, enrollments, grades and submissions may be possible.

    Two to eight weeks: audit, instance build in your region, SSO/SIS/LTI setup, course import and validation, training, cutover in a semester break, then enhanced support into term.

    • 3–4 weeks: 100+ courses, a light refresh, some integrations.
    • 4–8 weeks: full course upgrades, integrations and some custom work.
